4. MASTER TEACHER ANSWER KEY & EXPLANATIONS (Q1 – Q50)
================================================================================

--- PART 1: MULTIPLE CHOICES (Q1–Q10) ---
Q1 : Before (A)
     "Before I submit my work, I usually review it for errors."
     Explanation: Reviewing happens prior to submission.
Q2 : so that (D)
     "She stayed in the classroom so that she could finish her homework."
     Explanation: Expresses purpose.
Q3 : In spite of (D)
     "In spite of the heavy traffic, we still arrived on time."
     Explanation: Followed by a noun phrase ("the heavy traffic").
Q4 : Whenever (A)
     "You can use my computer whenever you need it."
     Explanation: Means "every time that" or "at any time that."
Q5 : despite (B)
     "I managed to finish the report despite being very tired."
     Explanation: "Despite + gerund (-ing)" is grammatically correct.
Q6 : even though (A)
     "The training continued even though several participants had raised concerns..."
     Explanation: Concessive conjunction introducing a finite clause.
Q7 : until (A)
     "The personnel were instructed to remain at the designated location until further instructions were issued."
     Explanation: Indicates time endpoint.
Q8 : Regardless of (A)
     "Regardless of the circumstances surrounding the operation, all personnel were expected to comply..."
     Explanation: Followed by a noun phrase ("the circumstances...").
Q9 : owing to (A)
     "The exercise was postponed owing to the deterioration in weather conditions."
     Explanation: Prepositional phrase followed by a noun phrase.
Q10: so that (B)
     "The commander revised the training schedule so that all personnel would have sufficient time..."
     Explanation: Expresses purpose with modal "would have".

Q21: CORRECT (A)   — "Although + clause" used accurately.
Q22: INCORRECT (B) — "Despite" cannot directly precede a finite clause.
Q23: CORRECT (A)   — "Unless + clause" represents a valid negative condition.
Q24: CORRECT (A)   — "Until + clause" with consistent past tense.

--- PART 4: WORD BANK PASSAGE COMPLETION (Q31–Q40) ---
Word Bank Choices: Because, Although, If, While, Since, Unless, After, Before, Until, Whereas
Distractors: so, As long as

Paragraph 1: Understanding Coordinating Conjunctions
Q31: Because (A)
     Explanation: Introduces the causal reason why these conjunctions are unique.
Q32: Although (B)
     Explanation: Establishes a concession between difficulty and the FANBOYS solution.
Q33: If (C)
     Explanation: Sets up a pure conditional requirement.
Q34: While (D)
     Explanation: Direct, simultaneous comparison between two differing elements.
Q35: Since (E)
     Explanation: Time conjunction meaning "from that specific time until now" with year 1900.

Paragraph 2: Punctuation and Rules
Q36: Unless (F)
     Sentence: "Writers will not combine two short clauses into one compound sentence unless they want to avoid choppy writing and improve the text's flow."
     Explanation: Introduces a negative condition meaning "except if," perfectly matching the negative main clause ("will not combine").
Q37: After (G)
     Sentence: "It is important to know exactly where to place a comma after a dependent clause is written first in a complex structure."
     Explanation: Chronological sequence for placing punctuation after introductory clauses.
Q38: Before (H)
     Sentence: "Always proofread your punctuation carefully before you finalize your essay and submit it to your teacher."
     Explanation: Checking must take place prior to final submission.
Q39: Until (I)
     Sentence: "You should keep practicing these connection rules every single day until you feel completely confident using them."
     Explanation: Specifies the temporal endpoint of continuous practice.
Q40: Whereas (J)
     Sentence: "Whereas coordinating conjunctions must sit directly between the clauses they connect, subordinating conjunctions can flexibly move to the front of the sentence."
     Explanation: Formal contrastive conjunction weighing two opposite grammatical behaviors.
